A button swallowed by ink

A rounded slab rests on a thin, heavy liquid. Hover and it tips under the pointer's weight like a plank with a ball on it. Click and it goes under with a splash: droplets burst from the rim, fly, strand and pinch off, then are drawn back onto the slab and merge until the mass has closed over it. Let go and they slide off and thin away as it bobs back up. Disable it and it stays under. Everything you see is one fragment shader over a fullscreen triangle generated from gl_VertexID — liquid, slab, meniscus and glitter alike. The control on top is a real <button>: Tab to it, press Enter or Space, and it sinks. Sunk, it keeps aria-disabled rather than disabled, so it stays findable and can say why it is unavailable.

Low viscosity, high tension

  • Hover tips, click dunks — the pointer is a ball rolling on a plank: the edge under it dips and the far edge lifts, rocking before it settles, but the slab stays afloat. A click sends it fully under for a beat, then it bobs back.
  • Underdamped spring — at viscosity 0.4 the slab overshoots and jiggles before it settles, the way a mercury bead quivers.
  • Particle-fluid swallow — ported from the ink-splat experiment. On impact ~100 droplets are launched from the slab's rim (drag, weak surface tension, all in JS) and rendered as a metaball density field, so strands, necks, bulbs and pinch-offs come out of the motion rather than being drawn. As the slab sinks the droplets are pulled onto it and swell until they merge into one mass; released, they slide off and thin. Droplet size, density and heap height are tweakable. The slab is never re-shaded — the mass occludes it and it fades beneath.
  • Mercury body — a third liquid, alongside mineral and pearl: a chrome reflection of a soft studio — bright above, dark below, a hard horizon band, tight speculars.
  • Transition on sink — with sinkmercury set, mineral or pearl turn to mercury as the slab goes under and back as it rises. Afloat, they keep their own look.
  • Fast ripples — thinner liquid means quicker, higher-frequency, longer-lived rings; thicker liquid slows and damps them.

The pointer dents a narrow, deep dimple rather than a wide bowl, and sheds a wake behind it.

 The label is DOM text over the canvas, so it stays crisp and selectable. Every twelfth frame the component reads the rendered pixels behind it, picks its ink by APCA, and — if the liquid has darkened past legibility — fades in exactly enough scrim to clear Lc 78. Prefers-reduced-motion: surface drift and facet twinkle freeze, ripples drop to a third. The sink and the rise remain — they carry state.
